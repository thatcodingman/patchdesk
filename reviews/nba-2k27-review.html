Worth the price?

NBA 2K27's price structure is unchanged from last year: $69.99 Standard, $99.99 Deluxe, $149.99 Ultra (Ultra's bonus content is limited through September 6). No increase, but no discount for a game that shipped needing three patches in its first ten days.

The community read on value split along a clear line: people genuinely like what's new, but aren't convinced it's worth full price on day one specifically.

"
It's cool it has MyCareer Eras so you can play in other eras of the NBA, I like that a lot but I'd prob wait till it's like on sale for $40 or $20.
NBA 2K27 player — Discord, anonymized

That's the honest tension running through this whole review: the additions are real, but the launch execution gives plenty of reasons to wait.

How it compares to last year

This isn't a reskin. 2K officially confirms a real gameplay overhaul: redesigned hands-up defense, expanded defensive cutoffs, a new collision system, and a full shot-contest rework. Layups get a Dynamic Layup Engine. MyCAREER adds five playable Eras (Magic/Bird 1984, Jordan 1991, Kobe 2003, LeBron 2010, Steph 2016) plus the first-ever co-op MyCAREER campaign, more than double the length of last year's story.

Community reaction to how it actually plays is split — and split in an interesting way, because the disagreement isn't about whether 2K27 is different, it's about whether different is better.

"
27 for randoms not being able to shoot, 26 for the takeovers.
NBA 2K27 player — Discord, anonymized
"
27, there was 0 skill gap in 26 — although BS layups are taking away from the skill gap a bit right now.
NBA 2K27 player — Discord, anonymized

One respondent gave us the most detailed side-by-side comparison we collected, and it's worth quoting at length because it captures a real trade-off rather than a simple "better" or "worse":

"
For 2K26 it was a quick moving, more casual 2K where builds could do everything, make any shots, a lot of dunks had high success rates — but it was a pretty decent game, if they buffed defense it would have been perfect. You could dribble fast pace, no RNG, build specialization didn't take all year to complete, same as crew level. This year it's RNG-based on the various catch/gather animations in this game that users can't time or prepare for, while also forcing players to have slower jumpshots for the green timing — where in any other 2K it wasn't a real reason to have a 50% shot over a 100% speed shot — too many different animations. This 2K seems to want to be slower, less rewarding for defense considering hands up being nerfed and slowing you down. Break-starter passing isn't nearly as effective as last year, RNG shooting, defense is weak besides blocks and bumps but actually getting a hand in the face will never be a heavy contest — seems like ratings don't matter that much for perimeter or interior defense, along with being able to get standing dunks being rare even if your attributes are high and allow them. People are making crazy shots this year, same as last, kind of crazy to think about.
NBA 2K27 player — Discord, anonymized

Read closely, this respondent's complaint isn't really "2K27 changed too much" — it's that the changes didn't land where the community wanted them. Last year's biggest gap, in their telling, was weak defense on an otherwise fast, skill-driven game. This year, the pace slowed down and RNG-feeling shot variance increased, but the specific complaint from last year — contested shots not actually playing like contests — is described as still present. That's a useful, specific version of "vs. last year" that goes beyond a simple thumbs up or down.

Badges and Crew progression

The badge system saw a real structural change too — a Synergy system and dynamic Badge Loadouts replace last year's simpler +1/+2 badge slots. Reaction here was mixed rather than negative:

"
I like the +1 and +2 badges much better than the synergy slots we have now. However, I like the badge point system and selecting which badges to apply it to — it makes you consider how much you're upgrading a category on a build to make sure you have enough points to upgrade whatever badges you need in said category. It's the same as old 2Ks like 22 and 23.
NBA 2K27 player — Discord, anonymized

On Crew progression specifically: 2K officially states City's 40 REP tiers pay out double the rewards over the same period as last year, and a mid-launch patch (v1.3) explicitly tuned Crew XP and REP earn rates based on community feedback. Even accounting for that, players on the ground still described rep gains as feeling slow — a case where the official numbers and the felt experience don't fully line up yet.

What got added, what got cut

▲ Added

  • Five playable MyCAREER Eras + first-ever co-op MyCAREER
  • Overhauled defense, shot contests, and collision system
  • Dynamic Layup Engine
  • First co-ed City, Crew HQ, Casual Corner, Daily Rivals
  • First-ever female MyPLAYER creation; Synergy badge system
  • 20+ community-requested MyNBA updates, four-team trades, 100-year Legacy universe
  • Unified cross-platform MyTEAM Auction House

▼ Cut

  • No official removal list was found. We're not calling anything "cut" on the strength of it being absent from marketing materials alone — e.g. 2K26's Pro-Am Summit doesn't appear in 2K27's City materials, but that's not confirmation it was actually removed.

Performance & bugs

This is where the launch got rough. 2K shipped three patches in the first ten days: v1.2 (Aug. 28) covered general performance/stability plus Auction House search and PC key-binding fixes; v1.3 (Aug. 31) fixed a Fire & Concrete co-op progression blocker, a City stats issue, a Rec hang, an Auction House hang, and matchmaking squad persistence; v1.4 (Sep. 3) added City stability fixes, fixed a MyCAREER FIBA offseason blocker, and added PS5 Edge controller support on Steam. 2K's own v1.4 developer note says a broader patch is planned before Season 2 — an acknowledgment, officially, that this isn't fully sorted yet.

Independent testing backs that up: Beebom's September 4 review documented shader compilation stutter and two crashes during PC testing, one during shader building specifically, plus heavy reliance on frame generation to hit target performance.

Community-reported issues tracked closely with that pattern. Crashes were reported in Theater mode specifically by one player (four times), and separately, frequent infinite loading screens and freezes were reported specifically in Park — tied to matches where an AI is guarding a real player, occurring across every game mode according to that respondent.

"
Once you have an AI on you, you're completely shut down for the entire game — you can't dribble against them, you can't shoot against them, you can't grab rebounds because of the box out. They shoot like 80% from three.
NBA 2K27 player — Discord, anonymized

Multiple players separately reported repeated disconnects and stability issues in online modes — described as happening more than once to the same players, not isolated one-off incidents. We could not find an official 2K statement confirming a widespread server outage, so we're not characterizing this as a universal launch-server failure — but it's a real, recurring pattern in what we heard directly, not an isolated complaint.

A separate, specific pattern showed up around exiting activities early: one player described getting hit with a 2-hour ban after leaving Theater mode following roughly 20 games, and a second player confirmed the same thing happening in MyTEAM.

"
Bro why tf do I keep getting banned for leaving theater — like I'll play 20 games and then stop, and it just bans me for 2 hours.
NBA 2K27 player — Discord, anonymized

A second respondent, replying to the same thread, said they were seeing the identical pattern in MyTEAM and summarized the launch bluntly: "this release is bad with servers, and online game modes." We can't confirm from the outside whether this is an anti-AFK system misfiring against players who genuinely finished a long session, or working as designed but tuned too aggressively — either way, it's a distinct issue from the general disconnect reports above, and one we'd flag as worth an official fix regardless of intent.

Worth disclosing directly: this is also something I've experienced firsthand as the author of this review. I received a week-long ban from 2K's official Discord for talking the same way everyone else in the server does, while users who openly promote VC glitches and scams post daily without apparent consequence. I can't speak to 2K's internal moderation criteria, and one person's ban doesn't establish a pattern — but it's a firsthand data point on enforcement feeling inconsistent, sitting alongside the in-game ban complaints above, and it felt important to disclose plainly rather than write around it.

Community members also flagged that some players are losing entire builds for no clear reason — an issue reportedly present since Early Access and still unresolved as of this review.

The verdict

NBA 2K27 is a genuine gameplay upgrade, not a roster update with a new coat of paint — the defense, contest, and layup systems are meaningfully different from last year, and MyCAREER's Eras concept is the kind of new idea that's actually landed well with the players we heard from. But the launch itself has been rough: real crashes, real repeated connectivity issues in online modes, and a progression grind that doesn't fully match 2K's own "double the rewards" framing yet — even with a mid-launch tuning patch already trying to close that gap.

The verdict

Worth playing, not necessarily worth buying at full price this week. The systems changes are real and mostly well-received — but between the crashes, the AI defense complaints, and players describing this as a "wait for a sale" purchase themselves, this reads like a game that'll be in a much better place after Season 2's promised broader patch than it is right now.
