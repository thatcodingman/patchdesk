Chorus of Sin Brings Fairer Matches, But The Judgment Misses

What changed

▲ Added / changed

  • New Killer: The Judgment — Divine Light and the Exile mechanic
  • New Survivor: Aurora
  • MMR overhaul — matchmaking now factors in-trial actions, not just kills/escapes
  • Teabagging now receives an in-game punishment

▼ Nerfed

  • Sprint Burst
  • Deliverance
  • Vigil — originally nerfed further, then partially walked back to reduce all status effects by 40% instead of Exhaustion only

Was this actually asked for?

Split, and it splits cleanly along which part of the patch you're asking about. The MMR overhaul addresses a matchmaking complaint the community has raised for a long time — but "addressing a long-standing complaint" isn't the same as "landing well on arrival." One respondent, a Killer main, described their early MMR experience as wildly inconsistent: roughly ten consecutive wins as Ghostface before the system started throwing tougher Survivors at them. A second respondent, who plays both sides, had a more measured take — obviously unbalanced matches still happen, but became noticeably less frequent the longer they played, suggesting the system does catch up, just not immediately. That respondent also made a point of separating old habits from new ones: their own 3–4 kill results as Killer weren't new to this patch at all — that gap, in their account, had always existed.

The perk nerfs landed better. Both respondents treated Sprint Burst, Deliverance, and Vigil's nerfs as reasonable, though the Vigil situation has a wrinkle: Behaviour partially walked back its own nerf, leaving it reducing all status effects by 40% instead of scoping it down to Exhaustion only — which one respondent flagged as the wrong call.

"
Yes, I'm getting absolute potatoes in the MMR. The Sprint Burst, Deliverance, and Vigil nerfs are all needed — but I don't like that they backpedaled on Vigil and made it reduce all status effects again instead of just Exhaustion. Haven't played The Judgment yet — I bought the killer for the perks.
Killer main — Discord, anonymized

The Judgment itself is where the community reaction turns critical, and it wasn't something players were specifically asking for in the form it shipped. Divine Light activating roughly every 3 seconds — with no way to direct it toward the Killer instead of only away, barring add-ons — reads to both respondents as either undertuned or awkwardly designed. The Exile mechanic fares better; both respondents called it fun and effective at slowing games down. But one respondent's read on why Exile works so well is less flattering to Divine Light specifically: it can feel like Exile is compensating for a Killer power that isn't strong enough on its own — a kit with an interesting secondary mechanic propping up an underwhelming core power, rather than two halves that are actually balanced against each other.

"
Judgment does not feel as well as I had hoped. The inability to direct Divine Light towards the killer (without addons) instead of strictly away feels unnatural. The Exile mechanic is fun, and it's excellent when it comes to slowing down games. However, it feels like a mechanic that balances out a weak killer power, i.e. Divine Light.
Plays both sides — Discord, anonymized

One more asymmetry both respondents raised independently: teabagging now gets punished, but slugging doesn't. Neither treated it as a dealbreaker, but both flagged it as an inconsistency worth Behaviour addressing if taunting behavior is now a target for punishment at all.

"
The game does still seem fair, albeit slightly killer sided, but I feel that is for the best. 2 survivors escaping feels best for all parties involved, although I consistently kill 3 or 4 during my killer matches.
Plays both sides — Discord, anonymized

Value check

The responses we collected don't give us enough to make a reliable price/value call on Chorus of Sin as a purchase. What we do have: one Killer main told us they bought The Judgment primarily for the perks it unlocks, not for the Killer's power itself — which lines up with how lukewarm both respondents were on Divine Light specifically.

File size & rollout

Chorus of Sin went live August 25, 2026, as DLC across PC, PlayStation, Xbox, and Nintendo Switch. Behaviour hasn't published an official per-platform download size for this chapter, so — consistent with how we handled THE FINALS and Sea of Thieves when the same information wasn't available — we're not publishing an estimated number. No widespread rollout issues were reported by either respondent.

The long view

Chorus of Sin reads as a mixed step forward, not a clean win. The MMR system shows real promise — matches becoming less frequently unbalanced the more a player logs in is exactly the trend you'd want to see from a matchmaking overhaul — but it can take a substantial win streak before the system actually catches up, which is a rough first impression even if the destination is right.

The Judgment is the bigger open problem. Exile is doing real work as a slowdown mechanic, but if that's propping up a Divine Light that both respondents independently found weak or awkward, that's a kit that isn't balanced against itself yet — it's balanced by one half compensating for the other. That's usually a sign of more tuning still to come, not a finished kit.

Compared to recent updates we've covered, this one trends slightly up, but unevenly — the matchmaking direction is promising even if the early experience is rough, and The Judgment simply hasn't landed as strongly as its perks alone might suggest.

The verdict

Chorus of Sin isn't a bad update, but it doesn't fully solve the problems it set out to fix. The MMR changes show real promise once they settle in. The Judgment needs more work — Exile is good, Divine Light isn't pulling its weight yet.
