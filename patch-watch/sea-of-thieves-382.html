What changed

▲ Added / changed

  • Faction Battle winners now get their ship fully repaired automatically
  • Custom Seas expands with the Seas of Sailor's Grave legendary location
  • New Custom Seas commands: Invulnerability, Ship Repair, Hide Pirate, negative weapon damage (heals allies), Skeleton Lord spawning, Reset Supplies
  • Rowboat server limit raised to 24, with new Cannon and Harpoon variants
  • "Big Ship, Little Ship" event (Aug 20–24) — Sloop-only matchmaking servers
  • Silver Coin weapon set added to Outpost shops for gold
  • New PC Streaming Detection to auto-suggest Streamer Mode settings

▼ Removed / fixed

  • Fixed a bug where bailed water incorrectly returned to the ship
  • Fixed PS5 invite crashes
  • Fixed "Glassbeard" errors when switching Pirate Profiles for Faction Battles
  • Fixed an indefinite "Finding Crew Ledger" hang when joining a session

What did the community feedback we found say?

The Faction Battle auto-repair has a clear practical benefit for players who have won a fight but can still sink afterward. Our community feedback was positive overall, although one Hourglass player preferred the LSS-style instant-removal approach instead. The Custom Seas expansion is a substantial development investment, but the feedback we collected did not show it was a major community priority. The Sloop-only "Big Ship, Little Ship" event sits in between — some players clearly wanted relief from crew-size mismatches, but there's no consensus that a permanent version is wanted.

"
If you are being 3rd partied and win, suddenly your boat is repaired and the 3rd party still has damage, creating an unfair scenario for the 3rd party who does not get any free repairs. It also makes risky plays more viable, meaning you might be more willing to go for a board play, or be willing to ignore bailing entirely to try to put maximum pressure on your opponent to beat them before you sink instead of playing responsibly.
Anonymous — Hourglass/Faction Battle player, community feedback

The same respondent argued Rare's Last Ship Standing approach — immediately removing a sunk ship from the game — would be a better fix, pointing to the game's "Tools not rules" philosophy: in their view, automatic repair functions as a rule rather than a tool.

"
Automatic repairs good because sometimes youll sink and lose all ur progress even though you won (they dont get anything if you sink after) custom seas is neat and good for training or YT thats about it.
Anonymous — Sea of Thieves player, community feedback

Asked what would make Custom Seas useful beyond training, the same respondent said its biggest practical benefit is simply getting into another fight faster — messing around with friends is possible, but training remains what they see as its best use.

Neither respondent thinks the LSS-style instant-removal take is the dominant Hourglass opinion — "it's not the most popular" — but it's not fringe either, and most players reportedly have no strong opinion either way since most don't touch Hourglass at all.

One respondent's theory on why Custom Seas launched without features like rowboat spawning — their own speculation, not a confirmed explanation from Rare: "Almost certainly a lack of having things prepared... Each island is sequestered away on its own area. Nothing about them has to work with the live servers... instead we are being drip fed because they probably don't have the capacity to actually do real work."

File size & rollout

Rare has published exact per-platform download sizes for previous updates, but a reliable 3.8.2 figure couldn't be verified at time of writing — so we're not publishing a number rather than estimate one from past patches. No widespread rollout failure was verified. Rare's known-issues material did identify Custom Seas lobby/roster problems.

The long view

3.8.2 is a genuinely substantial update — it's just not substantial in the direction most players will notice day to day. The Faction Battle repair change is a clean quality-of-life fix, although the feedback we collected suggests it may also change how aggressively players approach the final moments of a fight. The bulk of the update's weight, though, went into Custom Seas: a sandbox mode that's clearly valuable to creators, trainers, and YouTubers represented in the feedback we collected, but its day-to-day relevance to ordinary High Seas players remains less clear. That's not a criticism of the feature itself, but it does suggest the changelog looks bigger than what most players will actually feel.

The Sloop-only matchmaking event is the most interesting long-term signal here — it directly addresses crew-size mismatch in practice, and its reception could help show whether players want an optional permanent mode. Worth checking back on once the event wraps Aug 24.

The verdict

A meaningful systems patch, not a meaningful content patch for most players. The Faction Battle repair fix is an easy win. The real story is Custom Seas continuing to grow into its own separate game-within-a-game — great if that's your thing, mostly invisible if it isn't.
